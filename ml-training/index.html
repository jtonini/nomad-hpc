    Training the model from the Console¶

    From nomad 1.45.0 (with the Console's fix 110), an administrator trains the model from Data readiness ("Train…"). The training runs not on the hub but on a worker machine with a GPU (at Richmond, badenpowell), with TESSERA (tessera-ml 0.3 or later).

    How it goes¶

    1. An administrator asks on Data readiness. The request waits on the hub, in ml.db (beside lab_check.db, the nomad account's), with what it is to do ([ml.train] as it was then). One request at a time.
    2. The worker asks the Console every 5 minutes whether there is one (nomad ml work, from a systemd timer). It speaks to the Console over HTTPS with its own key; nothing logs in to the worker, and it logs in to nothing.
    3. It downloads the jobs' request-time figures, trains TESSERA and a logistic regression on the older jobs, scores the newest (held out) and those running or waiting, and sends back how well each did and the scores.
    4. Data readiness shows the result; nomad ml status on the hub too.

    What leaves the hub¶

    Per job, only what Slurm was asked at submission, as numbers: cores, GPUs, memory, time asked, the hour and weekday it was submitted, its partition and cluster numbered (not named), the month it was submitted; and what became of it. Never a person, a job's name, a job's ID or a command line. The hub keeps which row was which job.

    Jobs: those submitted in the last jobs_days that ended (COMPLETED, FAILED, TIMEOUT, OUT_OF_MEMORY, NODE_FAIL; a cancelled or preempted job is a decision, not an outcome), at most the newest max_jobs; and those running or waiting.

    What it predicts (predicts):

    • failure: an ended job that didn't complete;
    • idle (1.46.0, the default with failure): a completed job of 2 cores or more that ran 5 minutes or more and was measured, which on average left more than idle_threshold of the cores it asked for unused: the jobs the Recommendations judge for cores, judged as they judge them;
    • waste: a completed job that used less than 1 - waste_threshold of the time it asked for. Where most jobs don't set --time and get their partition's default, nearly every job counts (at the University of Richmond, half the completed jobs used under 1% of the time they asked, and 89% would count even at 0.9): the question then says little, which is why it is no longer asked by default.

    How well it did, honestly¶

    The newest holdout_share of the ended jobs (by when they ended) are given to TESSERA as unknown (label -1): in its similarity network, but learned from by nothing. Only afterwards are its scores for them compared with what happened, and with the scores of a logistic regression trained on the same older jobs. Data readiness shows, per target:

    • AUC: how often a model ranks a job that did ahead of one that didn't (0.5 is chance, 1 always), for TESSERA and the logistic regression;
    • of the tenth ranked riskiest, how many did, against how many of all held-out jobs did;
    • TESSERA's blend: how much each of its parts (GNN, LSTM, autoencoders) counted, by how well each did on its own validation jobs;
    • with compare (1.45.1), each model's AUC without partition and cluster, and the logistic regression's with them alone: how much of what a model knows is where a job ran, rather than what it asked for;
    • for idle and waste, how much of the cores or the time they asked for the jobs used (at the tenth, quarter, half, three quarters and nine tenths of them) and how many would count at a few thresholds: the counts to choose idle_threshold and waste_threshold by. When nearly every job counts, the AUCs say little, and the card says so;
    • with bootstrap (1.46.0), the 95% range of each AUC and of the difference between the two models, from resamples of the held-out jobs (the same jobs for both models in each resample). Where the difference's range spans zero, the card says they do about as well, whatever their AUCs.

    When the logistic regression does as well or better, it says so.

    Settings¶

    On the hub, in nomad.toml (its own table: the older [ml] keys are another thing's):

    [ml.train]
enabled = true                   # the Console offers Train… (administrators)
engine = "tessera"               # or "baseline": a logistic regression only
predicts = ["failure", "idle"]   # or with "waste"
trainer = "badenpowell"          # the worker's name, as the Console says it
jobs_days = 365
max_jobs = 50000                 # the newest ended jobs (0: all); the similarity
                                 # network's time grows as the square of the jobs
waste_threshold = 0.6
idle_threshold = 0.5             # (1.46.0)
holdout_share = 0.2
bootstrap = 1000                 # resamples for the ranges (0: none) (1.46.0)
keep_runs = 20                   # runs kept with their scores
stale_minutes = 360              # a run not heard from this long is given up
key_file = "~/.config/nomad/ml_worker_key"   # the nomad account's (600)
# db = "ml.db"                   # default: beside lab_check.db
compare = true                   # also each model without partition and cluster, and the
                                 # logistic regression with them alone (1.45.1)

[ml.train.epochs]
gnn = 100
lstm = 60
ae = 60

[ml.train.tessera]               # (1.45.1)
similarity = "euclidean"         # or cosine, mahalanobis, pearson, bray_curtis (on scaled
                                 # features); simpson, jaccard (on n_bins equal-width bins)
n_bins = 3                       # simpson and jaccard only
k_neighbors = 10                 # each job's nearest neighbours in the network

    Both models get the same columns: what was asked, as numbers (cores, GPUs, memory and time on a log scale; the hour and day it was submitted), and partition and cluster as one column per value. In 1.45.0 TESSERA had partition and cluster as plain numbers, and its default similarity (simpson) cuts each feature into three equal-width bins between its smallest and largest value, so a partition, or a memory or time asked, was only low, middle or high. Each run keeps the settings it was asked with.

    On the worker:

    [ml.worker]
console = "https://nomad.example.edu"   # the Console it asks
key_file = "~/.config/nomad/ml_worker_key"
device = "cuda"                         # or "cpu", "auto"
# ca_file = "/etc/pki/tls/certs/campus-ca.pem"
# name = "badenpowell"                  # default: the machine's short name

    Setting it up¶

    On the hub, as the account the Console runs as, make the worker's key:

    sudo -u nomad nomad ml key

    On the worker, a Python environment with nomad and TESSERA (PyTorch with CUDA comes with them from PyPI), the key copied from the hub (never pasted anywhere else), its settings, a check, and the timer:

    sudo install -d -o "$USER" -g "$(id -gn)" -m 755 /opt/nomad-ml
python3 -m venv /opt/nomad-ml && /opt/nomad-ml/bin/pip install 'nomad-hpc[predict]'
sudo restorecon -R /opt/nomad-ml     # where SELinux is on: the default labels (bin/ is bin_t)
umask 077 && mkdir -p ~/.config/nomad && ssh you@hub 'sudo -n -u nomad cat /home/nomad/.config/nomad/ml_worker_key' > ~/.config/nomad/ml_worker_key
/opt/nomad-ml/bin/nomad -c ~/.config/nomad/ml_worker.toml ml key      # the same fingerprint as on the hub
/opt/nomad-ml/bin/nomad -c ~/.config/nomad/ml_worker.toml ml check    # reaches the Console; can it train here
sudo /opt/nomad-ml/bin/nomad -c ~/.config/nomad/ml_worker.toml ml timer --user $USER --apply

    The environment goes in /opt, not in a home folder: the timer runs it as a system service, and where SELinux is enforcing (Rocky, RHEL) a system service may not run programs from a home folder (user_home_t). It fails at once with status 203/EXEC and an AVC denial ("denied { execute }", init_t); /opt/*/bin has the label of system programs (bin_t). The key and the settings can stay in the home folder: the service only reads them.

    nomad ml check says what the Console answered (on or off, what is queued) and whether TESSERA can train on this machine: its version, PyTorch's, and the GPU it would use, or why not. The worker never follows a redirect: a sign-in page in front of the Console would otherwise be sent the key. If the web server in front of the Console asks for a campus sign-in, let /api/ml/worker/ through to the Console (it admits only the worker's key, and refuses every person), or point [ml.worker] console at an address the Console answers at itself.

    Commands¶

    nomad ml status [--json]                        the request queued or running, the last result (hub)
nomad ml ask [--note "..."]                     queue a request, as Train… does (hub)
nomad ml cancel ID                              a queued request (hub)
nomad ml scores ID failure|waste [--part now|holdout] [--top N]   a run's riskiest jobs (hub: names them)
nomad ml key                                    make (hub) or check (worker) the key: its fingerprint
nomad ml check                                  the worker reaches the Console with its key
nomad ml work                                   one pass: take a request, if any, train, answer
nomad ml timer [--apply]                        that, every 5 minutes (nomad-ml-worker.timer)
