    Condo reports¶

    A condo is a set of nodes a lab bought, lent to everyone else through a guest partition while the lab isn't using them. nomad usage-report condos writes each condo's monthly report for its owner, and a summary of every condo for research computing, grouped by cluster.

    nomad usage-report condos init               # draft the condos into report.toml
nomad usage-report condos init --apply       # ... and add them to it (in a terminal, it asks how reports go out)
nomad usage-report condos status             # what is left to set up
nomad usage-report condos set --labs mail_domain=example.edu --labs review_to=rc@example.edu \
                              --confirm smith,jones --owner lee=klee --apply
nomad usage-report condos                    # last month's reports, written
nomad usage-report condos --send             # ... and mailed
nomad usage-report condos --month 2026-09 --cluster c1 --condo smith
sudo nomad usage-report condos timer --apply # make and mail them on the 1st

    The Console does the same for admins, under Settings > Condo reports: the settings, the condos with nomad's owner guesses, a preview of each report, and sending a month for review.

    Setting it up¶

    nomad init asks whether to register condos: for each Slurm cluster, the guest partition (the one named like "condo" by default), then each condo it finds there with its owner's NetID (an owner typed there is confirmed; left blank, it is set later), the owners' mail domain and who reviews the reports first. It writes them to report.toml beside nomad.toml. The reports are made where a cluster's data is gathered: on that machine, or on the hub that syncs it (copy report.toml there, or set it up there as below).

    On a hub:

    1. nomad usage-report condos init --apply drafts every cluster's condos into report.toml, each owner guessed and not confirmed. In a terminal it then asks what a first setup needs and isn't set yet: the owners' mail domain, who reviews the reports first (review_to), the sender and the line at the foot (--no-input skips this).
    2. Check each owner and confirm it (condos set --confirm NAME --apply, or the Console).
    3. Read last month's reports (nomad usage-report condos). With review_to set, --send mails them all there; without it, --send mails each confirmed owner theirs.
    4. nomad usage-report condos timer --apply, as root, installs the timer that makes and mails them on the 1st.
    5. When the reviewed reports read right, clear review_to: from then on each goes to its owner.

    nomad usage-report condos status says what is left at any point:

    • condo partitions on the nodes that report.toml doesn't list (a new condo), and condos it lists that are gone from the nodes;
    • owners not confirmed (their reports don't go out);
    • confirmed condos without an address (no mail_domain, no email), and no sender;
    • the timer, not installed or not enabled;
    • notes: review mode (every report goes to review_to) and no PDF (fpdf2 missing).

    Most lines say what to run. nomad config check on a hub (or wherever report.toml lists condos) shows the same in counts, naming no condos or owners, and the Console lists it at the top of Settings > Condo reports.

    It reads the hub's combined database (--db names another) and the same report.toml as nomad usage-report (--config names another).

    What an owner gets¶

    Each report names the cluster it covers, in its title and on a badge, so an owner with condos on two clusters can tell them apart.

    1. The nodes: how many, their cores, memory and GPUs, and the partition.
    2. The month on the nodes: of the core-hours the nodes were in service, the share the owner's lab used, the share guests used, and the share nobody used, as a bar and as figures; the node-hours in service, down, drained, in maintenance and not read; how many guest jobs ran there, how many were preempted, and how many ran longer than the guest limit.
    3. The lab's jobs on its own nodes: of the jobs submitted to the condo's partition in the month, the share that started within the guarantee (owner_start_minutes, from when the job could start: Slurm's Eligible time, else its submission), whenever they started: a job submitted on the 30th that starts on the 1st is the month's. A job still waiting when the report is made counts as late once it has waited longer than the guarantee. A later start is put down to guest jobs on the nodes at the time, the lab's own jobs filling them, or neither (dependencies, a request larger than the nodes, nodes out of service). Then how the jobs that ended did (completed, failed, out of memory, timed out, cancelled) and how much of the cores and memory they held they used.
    4. The lab elsewhere: the lab's use of the cluster's shared nodes, of other condos as guests, and of other clusters: core-hours, GPU-hours, people, failures, CPU and memory used, and median wait.
    5. Members: each member of the lab who ran anything, by NetID, with their core-hours on the condo, on the shared nodes, on other condos and on other clusters (name_members); the others are counted. People who ran in the condo's partition without being members are listed too, marked, with their jobs there only. Guests on the owner's nodes are only ever totals.
    6. Storage: the lab's scratch quota and the home quota from [report.policy]. Not measured yet: once NØMAÐ reads quotas, this shows what is used.
    7. The past 12 months: the lab, guest and idle shares month by month.
    8. What the figures rest on: node-hours not read, jobs not measured, and what a figure can't count.

    A run limited by --cluster or --condo writes and sends no summary: a summary of some condos would stand for the month's.

    Research computing's summary has one table per cluster, a row per condo (owner, nodes, shares, guest core-hours, preemptions, how many owner jobs started within the guarantee, node-hours out of service) and a bar per condo. A cluster without condos is listed as having none. It also lists what needs attention: condo partitions on the nodes that report.toml doesn't list, condos whose partition is gone, and owners not confirmed.

    How the figures are made¶

    • Node-hours come from the node readings (every 5 minutes). Each reading stands for the time until the node's next one, up to twice the node's usual interval that month (the median gap, and at least 10 minutes); the rest is "not read", counted as in service, and the report says how much there was. The interval is each month's own, so a site that read its nodes more often earlier in the year isn't measured by that interval now. A node counts from its first reading only when the cluster's other nodes were reporting before it (a new node), and until its last only when they went on after it (a node retired). Months before a cluster's first reading have no readings.
    • Core-hours are each job's cores (Slurm's allocation, else the request) times the hours it ran inside the month, split evenly over its nodes. A job that left the queue with no outcome recorded (UNKNOWN) ran until NØMAÐ saw it gone. GPUs are the allocation's, or the request's where the site's accounting doesn't count GPUs.
    • Owner or guest: a job on the condo's nodes is the owner's when it ran in the condo's partition, under the lab's Slurm account (account), or for a member of the lab; any other job there is a guest's. Idle is what is left of the in-service core-hours.
    • Preempted guest jobs are those that ended PREEMPTED. A guest job that was requeued keeps its job number and only its last run is kept, so the count is a lower bound.
    • Members: members in report.toml (a Unix group, a Slurm account, or a list); else each owner's labs by [console.labs] in nomad.toml (the group named for them by group_pattern, and the groups they lead), and the owners.

    Finding the condos¶

    Nothing new runs at the sites: each node reading carries the node's partitions. The guest partition is the one named in guest_partitions, or one named like "condo". A condo is a partition whose nodes all sit inside the guest partition. A partition over every condo node is another way in for guests, unless it is the only condo there is.

    The owner is guessed from two kinds of evidence, because each alone misleads: a partition can be named for another of the PI's surnames, and a condo lent out a lot is used mostly by other labs.

    • The name. owner_patterns ({name}, {name}lab, {name}_lab and {name}-lab by default) give a last name, a number at the end being a second condo of the same lab (smith2). The owner is a NetID of up to two initials and that name, perhaps with a number after it (jsmith3), cut short where the institution's NetIDs stop (netid_max_length, 8: tanderso for Anderson): first one with a lab group named for it, then one that leads a lab in nomad.toml, then one that ran jobs in the partition, then an account. Several accounts that fit, none of them a lab's PI, are listed rather than guessed from. Two last names joined (smithjones) are two owners, when one of them at least is a lab's PI.
    • The users. The lab groups (by [console.labs] group_pattern) of the people who ran jobs in the partition over the past year, the one holding the most of them first. When it holds more than half of them (two at least), it names the owner where the name names nobody, or only an account.

    When the two point at different labs, the name's owner is kept and the draft says to check. init writes each guess with its evidence and confirmed = false; a report goes to its owners only once someone sets it to true.

    Changing the settings¶

    nomad usage-report condos set changes report.toml without opening it:

    • --labs KEY=VALUE and --policy KEY=VALUE: a [report.labs] or [report.policy] setting; lists as A,B, numbers and true/false as such;
    • --confirm A,B and --unconfirm A,B: which condos' reports may go to their owners;
    • --owner CONDO=NETID[,NETID]: a condo's owner, or owners.

    A condo is its name, or CLUSTER/NAME when two clusters have one of that name. Without --apply it shows the change and writes nothing. With it, the file is copied first and keeps its owner and permissions, and nothing is written unless the result reads as condo settings (a misspelled setting or an address that isn't one is refused). --mask prints counts instead of names.

    Delivery¶

    Reports are written to OUT/<cluster>/<condo>/<YYYY-MM>.{html,pdf,json,md} and OUT/summary/<YYYY-MM>.*, readable only by the account that writes them: they name people. OUT is out_dir in [report.labs], else reports/condos in nomad's data directory, else --out.

    The email is HTML made for mail readers (tables, inline styles), with a plain-text part and the PDF attached. Each report is headed like the Console's sign-in page, from [console.branding] in nomad.toml: the logo, the institution and the unit lines under it (unit = ["Research Computing", "Office of the Provost"]). The logo must be a PNG or JPEG at a full path; it goes inside the HTML file, inline in the email (so mail readers show it without fetching anything), and in the PDF. branding = false in [report.labs] leaves it all out; without [console.branding], institution in [report.labs] names the sender. The PDF needs fpdf2:

    pip install 'nomad-hpc[report]'

    Without it the reports are written and sent without a PDF, and the email says so.

    Mail goes through [mail] in nomad.toml. While review_to is set, every report goes there, marked with whom it would have gone to, and so does the summary when summary_to names someone else; clear it to send reports to their owners and the summary to summary_to. What was sent each month is kept in OUT/summary/<YYYY-MM>.sent.json, so a second run doesn't mail the same report twice (--resend does).

    On the first of each month¶

    nomad usage-report condos timer prints the timer for this install (the nomad in use, its nomad.toml, and the nomad account, or --user), and --apply (as root) installs and starts it; --at changes when (systemd's OnCalendar, the 1st at 07:00 by default, after the night's syncs). A timer already set up with the same settings is left as it is. It is this, on the hub:

    # /etc/systemd/system/nomad-condo-reports.service
[Unit]
Description=NOMAD condo reports
After=network-online.target

[Service]
Type=oneshot
User=nomad
ExecStart=/opt/nomad/venv/bin/nomad -c /etc/nomad/nomad.toml usage-report condos --send

# /etc/systemd/system/nomad-condo-reports.timer
[Unit]
Description=NOMAD condo reports, on the 1st

[Timer]
OnCalendar=*-*-01 07:00
Persistent=true

[Install]
WantedBy=timers.target

    sudo systemctl daemon-reload
sudo systemctl enable --now nomad-condo-reports.timer

    Settings¶

    Everything one institution may do differently goes in report.toml:

    [report.labs]                          # how the reports go out
mail_domain = "example.edu"            # owner's address: NETID@mail_domain
send_from = "Research Computing <rc@example.edu>"
review_to = ["rc@example.edu"]         # while set, every report goes here
summary_to = ["rc@example.edu"]        # the summary (default: review_to)
name_members = true                    # an owner's report names their lab's members
formats = ["html", "pdf", "json"]      # also "md"
owner_patterns = ["{name}", "{name}lab", "{name}_lab", "{name}-lab"]
netid_max_length = 8                   # NetIDs cut short here; 0: never
contact = "Questions: rc@example.edu"  # the line at the foot of each report
out_dir = "/var/lib/nomad/reports/condos"
institution = "Research Computing"     # the name at the top, without [console.branding]
branding = true                        # head reports with [console.branding]: logo and lines

[report.policy]                        # what the reports check against
owner_start_minutes = 5                # an owner's job starts on its own nodes within
guest_max_days = 10                    # the longest a guest job may run
guest_grace_minutes = 5
home_quota_gb = { soft = 50, hard = 60 }
lab_scratch_quota_tb = { soft = 20, hard = 24 }
scratch_path = "/scratch/{lab}"
use_below = 0.25                       # below this share of what jobs ask for, say so

[report.clusters.c1]
label = "Cluster 1"                    # how the reports name the cluster
guest_partitions = ["condos"]          # default: partitions named like "condo"

[report.clusters.c1.condos.smith]      # drafted by `nomad usage-report condos init`
owner = "jsmith"                       # or several: ["jsmith", "rjones"]
partition = "smith"                    # default: the table's name
nodes = "c[01-04]"                     # default: the partition's nodes
members = "group:smith$"               # or "account:smith", or a list of NetIDs
account = "smith"                      # the lab's Slurm account, if it has one
email = "jsmith@example.edu"           # or a list; default: each owner@mail_domain
cc = ["manager@example.edu"]
confirmed = true
lab_scratch_quota_tb = 30              # an agreed larger quota

    A cluster with a condos table is reported as listed there. One without is reported from what its nodes say, every condo unconfirmed, until init drafts it. A setting nobody reads is refused (reveiw_to would otherwise send reports to owners). A site's own database, with no hub, is the cluster this-cluster, or the one cluster of its nomad.toml that report.toml lists condos for (as nomad init writes them).
