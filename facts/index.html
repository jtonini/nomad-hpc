    Facts¶

    Three questions every front to nomad asks, answered once, as data:

    What it answers Who may ask, in the Console
    sites every site now (freshness, nodes, CPU, memory and GPUs in use, jobs running and waiting, storage, alerts, workstations) and its jobs over a window anyone signed in: aggregates only
    me one person's work: their jobs over a window, running and waiting now, score and advice that person
    lab the labs a person leads: members, their jobs taken together, the lab's workstations and storage the lab's PI; staff for any lab
    course the courses a person teaches, this term: the class together, each student as a row, when the class works the course's instructors; staff for any course
    busy when each cluster, interactive server and group of workstations is busiest: the most in use at once and when, a typical week, the busiest and quietest hours (1.14.7) anyone signed in; each workstation by name for staff only

    The Console serves them at /api/v1/sites, /api/v1/me and /api/v1/lab; a dashboard widget elsewhere (an Open OnDemand page, say) or an assistant answering questions reads the same answers, so no two of them can disagree. On the command line:

    nomad facts sites                 # every site, jobs over the last 30 days
nomad facts me NETID --days 90    # one person
nomad facts lab NETID             # the labs NETID leads
nomad facts lab NETID --each      # each member too, by NetID
nomad facts sites --json          # the data itself
nomad facts lab NETID --mask      # no NetID, lab, machine or storage names, for sharing
nomad facts course --instructor NETID   # the courses NETID teaches (needs [console.courses])
nomad facts course GROUP              # one course
nomad facts busy --days 90 --each     # when each machine is busiest, each workstation too

    They read the hub's combined.db when there is one, else this host's own database (--db to choose). Nothing is written.

    What every answer carries¶

    {
  "api": "nomad.facts/1",
  "kind": "sites",
  "generated_at": "2026-10-11T07:00:00",
  "window": {"days": 30, "since": "2026-09-11T07:00:00", "until": "2026-10-11T07:00:00"},
  "notes": ["..."]
}

    api changes only when a field changes meaning or goes away; new fields can appear at any time.

    Two rules hold throughout:

    • Nothing measured, no number. A share that rests on no measurement is null; "CPU used" is over the jobs whose CPU use nomad measured, and says how many those were (jobs_measured). Nothing is shown as 0 for lack of data.
    • Each site against its own latest reading. A site that has not reported for two hours is stale: its figures are what it last said, never averaged in as if current, and it is never dropped. Its nodes, GPUs and queue are judged by the node collector's own latest reading; jobs "running now" at a site that stopped reporting are listed, marked stale, and left out of the totals.
    • No figure that is one person's in disguise. Where the caller says so (the Console does, for anyone but staff), a site's job figures resting on 3 people or fewer, and a lab's totals (and how many members ran jobs) resting on fewer than 3 members besides the one asking, are left out ("suppressed": true and why). The Console also gives anyone but staff fixed windows (7, 30, 90 or 365 days), so answers a day apart can't be subtracted. That stops reading one person off one answer, not combining several: two windows, two labs, or a site's total and a lab's can still come down to one person. These figures are not anonymised; what a PI may see of each student is the institution's decision.

    Jobs, wherever they appear¶

    The same calculation for a site, a person and a lab, over the jobs that ended in the window:

    Field Meaning
    finished jobs that ended in the window
    outcomes completed, failed, timeout, out_of_memory, node_fail, cancelled, preempted, other
    ran_to_an_end finished, less cancelled, preempted and other
    failed_pct failed, timed out or out of memory, of those that ran to an end; node failures are the machine's and in neither count (the Console's Dashboard counts them as failures, of every job that ended, until it reads these facts)
    people how many people's jobs these are (a count, never names)
    core_hours.requested cores × run time asked for, all jobs
    core_hours.used_pct core-hours used over core-hours requested, over the jobs measured
    memory.used_pct memory at peak over memory requested, by memory-hours, over the jobs measured
    memory.set_aside peaks larger than any node at the job's site: not measurements (Slurm's jobacct_gather/linux counts shared memory once per process)
    gpus.jobs, gpus.gpu_hours_requested GPUs asked for; their use per job is not measured here
    wait_minutes.median, .p90 time from submission to start

    sites¶

    For each site (the hub's sync_sites, so a site whose sync fails is still listed):

    • status: ok, stale or no_data; last_data and age_minutes, from its newest reading in any table; sync.failing and sync.last_success on a hub (the reason stays in the hub's sync log).
    • For a Slurm site: nodes (up, drained, down, not reporting, and the unavailable ones; Slurm's reason for each only when asked for, since an admin's free text can name people: the Console gives it to staff), cpu and memory (allocated_pct: what Slurm has handed out, not what jobs use), gpus (busy_pct from DCGM, over the GPUs it measured: nvidia-smi's "utilization" is any kernel running at all, and is not used), queue (running and waiting now) and jobs over the window.
    • workstations: how many it collects and how many are online now.
    • storage: each filesystem read in the last week: size, use, how fast it is filling over 30 days (least squares over daily averages of the fall in free space, which also catches a shared pool filling while this filesystem's own use stays flat) and days until full at that rate; same_as when another site or path reports the same size and use.
    • alerts: conditions holding now, by severity (counts only: an alert's message can name things a viewer should not see).

    me¶

    person.username, jobs (as above), jobs_by_site, now (running and waiting, by site, with whether that site is stale), and score and advice: the same as nomad edu me and the Console's My Activity, from the same code.

    lab¶

    For each lab the person leads, by nomad.toml's rules ([console.labs], [console.storage]; the same labs as nomad console roles NETID and the Console's My Lab):

    • members: current members (seen in the groups collector's latest run, with an account), how many ran jobs in the window, how many the group lists without an account or no longer lists.
    • jobs and now: the members' jobs taken together.
    • workstations: the lab's machines, online or not, last seen.

    • storage: each server or export the lab has, with its size and use; shares_free_with for exports of one pool (their free space is one).

    • each (with names=True, --each on the command line): a row per member by NetID: jobs finished in the window, core-hours held and the share used, memory used of what was asked, GPU-hours, the median wait, and jobs running and waiting now. Most core-hours first.

    Lab-wide figures unless names are asked for. How much a PI sees of each student is the institution's decision: the Console gives each member's row to staff and to the lab's own PI, for that lab. Without names, no row is about one member. A member's jobs count wholly toward the lab, and someone in two labs counts in both: group membership is all there is to go on.

    course¶

    For each course the person teaches this term, by nomad.toml's [console.courses] (see docs/config.md, Courses):

    • students: the course's people who are not faculty (listed: its group's members, and whoever used its environments this term, on two days or more); this term's (this_term: joined since the term began, or used an interactive server or a cluster since; for a course whose environment's users are mostly not in its group, by_environment: joined, or used the environment); those left out, as from earlier terms (nomad saw them join before) or not known (in the group since before nomad 1.11 recorded join dates, and not active: earlier students, or some who haven't started); used_a_server, not_yet, and open_never_computing (had sessions open while computing was measured, and never computed: possibly stuck).
    • interactive: hours open and computing for the class, week by week, and from when each was recorded.
    • each_student: one row per student, busiest first: hours open in a typical week (the median over the term's complete weeks) and the range of their weeks (10th to 90th percentile), the same for hours computing over the weeks it was measured, CPU-hours, peak memory, memory kills, weeks active. This is what a caterpillar plot draws: one row per student, a dot for the typical week, a bar for the range. nomad facts course prints it. Rows are "student 1", "student 2"... unless [console.courses] names is true (the institution's decision).
    • when: students on, and computing, on average at each hour of each weekday (from nomad 1.11.1's hourly totals).
    • oom: the students' sessions the kernel killed for lack of memory.
    • jobs: their cluster jobs, taken together.

    Each course says who teaches it and why (instructors_why: added by name, confirmed (they said so this term), only faculty member among its people, joined its group this term, environment: used its environment this term, active this term where its students work, those sites in active_on; see Courses in config.md), and how many of its faculty members were left out or said no. environments lists the environments whose users this term are among its people (nomad 1.12.5). Asked for every course, one nobody teaches this term is left out and listed in not_shown with the reason. answers (from nomad.config.course_answers.load) is what faculty members said this term.

    For the Console's questions: facts.courses.questions(conn, config, netid, answers) lists the courses that have netid as a faculty member, in its group or a user of the environment named for it (with students this term, or already answered): their answer and whether nomad takes them as teaching it; facts.courses.may_answer(conn, config, netid, group) says whether they may answer for a course. facts.teaches(conn, config, netid, answers) names the courses a person teaches without their figures (a menu), and facts.every_course(conn, config) every course this term, for staff to choose from.

    For whoever sets courses up: facts.courses.discover(conn, config) (what nomad course discover prints) lists the environments people had a session in this term (people, faculty members among them, the courses each counts for, whether it is named like a course, the group most of its other users are in) and the groups that are not courses with a faculty member and people active this term (whose own group each is, the environment most of them used), with how many courses there are and how many nobody teaches. It names NetIDs, groups and environments.

    A week before a figure was recorded is left out of it, never counted as zero: hours open from nomad 1.11 (earlier weeks once nomad interactive backfill has rebuilt them), computing from 1.11, hours of the week from 1.11.1. With min_students set, a course with fewer students this term shows counts only. Unnamed rows are not anonymous to an instructor who knows the class: in a course of three, the rows are three people.

    busy¶

    For each cluster, interactive server and group of workstations over the window: the most in use at once and when, the average, a typical week (each weekday's hour averaged over the window) and the three busiest and quietest hours. Machines only, never a person. Workstations go by lab: a machine [console.labs.resources] lists for a lab, or its collector tags with the lab's group (its latest department, as for My Lab), is the lab's, in every lab that lists or tags it; machines in no lab go by the site that collects them. A machine read by two sites counts once. Anything nomad.toml lists as storage (a lab's storage, [console.storage] servers and their names, the storage collector's devices) is left out, even when the workstation collector reads it too; names match in any case and without their domain (a NAS another site collects needs its [console.storage] entry on the hub). Machines and labs are listed by name (each, label), and both can be named for people, so the Console shows a lab to staff and to its own PI only, and --mask numbers them. CPUs are as each system counts them: Slurm's CPUs on the clusters, and a hardware thread on servers and workstations (two per core with hyperthreading).

    • Clusters (node_state): cores allocated (CPUAlloc) of the cores of the nodes in service. A node down, not responding, failing, in maintenance, or drained with nothing running is out of service: left out of the totals and counted apart (nodes_out: on average and at most).
    • Load (CPULoad): each node's counts up to its cores, for the most at once as for the average. Load counts threads ready to run as well as running, and processes waiting on storage, so a node whose storage stalls can read far above its cores; over_cores_pct and over_cores_readings say how often a node read above its cores. Where jobs are held to their cores (task/cgroup), that means jobs started more threads than cores they asked for, or were waiting on storage. Load is not cores in use.
    • Memory allocated (AllocMem), or "tracked": false where Slurm doesn't count memory (SelectTypeParameters without _Memory), never 0%.
    • Memory held: on the nodes running jobs, each node's memory less what Slurm reports free, as a share of the memory of all the nodes in service. Slurm's free memory is the kernel's free memory (MemFree), which counts the file cache as used, so this is the most the jobs could have used: jobs used this much or less. Nodes running nothing count as none (whatever they hold is cache, or left behind, not a job's). A free figure of 0 (Slurm's FreeMem=N/A, from a node that hasn't reported) is not counted.
    • A cluster whose every reading in the window had every node out of service has no figures; it is listed in clusters_all_out instead.
    • Readings that aren't numbers (text in a number column) are not counted.
    • Interactive servers (interactive_hourly): people with a session open (the most in an hour, and the hour's average), people computing, the memory their sessions held, and the machine's own busy cores (mpstat_summary) where the collector runs on it (one server at the site).
    • Workstations: the cores people's sessions kept busy, from each person's CPU counters (workstation_user_snapshot, accounts from uid 1000): everything in their user slice, logins, terminals and cron jobs; the machine's own services are not included. Memory in use is the machine's, cache left out (workstation_state). A machine is busy with a core or more in use. The group's figures add each machine's ten-minute averages (machines aren't read at the same instant). The workstation collector's own CPU percentages were never measured, and are not used. A machine whose per-person probe read only its own accounts (the collector's root login, the login screen: uid below 1000) reads 0, nobody used it; one with no per-person readings at all says its CPU is not measured.

    An hour of the week is ranked busiest or quietest only with readings on two different days or more, and nothing is ranked when every hour is the same. A site whose last reading is more than two hours old is marked stopped. Times are as the collectors wrote them: each site's local time.

    Using the functions¶

    from nomad import facts
conn = facts.open_readonly("~/.local/share/nomad/combined.db")
facts.sites(conn, days=30, config=config)
facts.person(conn, "jdoe", days=30, config=config, db_path=path)   # db_path: score and advice
facts.labs(conn, config, "pi1", days=30, min_others=3)             # what a PI is given
facts.labs(conn, config, groups=["pi1$"])                            # staff, by group
facts.sites(conn, reasons=True)                                      # staff: node reasons
facts.busy(conn, days=30, names=False)                               # machines numbered, not named

    The functions decide nothing about who may ask: the caller does.
